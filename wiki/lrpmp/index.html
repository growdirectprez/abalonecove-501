RPV Long-Range Property Management Plan (LRPMP)

RPV Long-Range Property Management Plan (LRPMP)

The LRPMP is the state-mandated plan governing disposition of all real property formerly owned by the Rancho Palos Verdes Redevelopment Agency (RDA). It is the foundational document behind all 11 parcels now owned by the City in the Abalone Cove / Portuguese Bend area.

Why the RDA Existed

The RPV Redevelopment Agency was formed in 1984 for Project Area No. 1 — the ancient landslide complex. Its purpose was to finance long-term capital improvements to stabilize the Portuguese Bend Landslide (active since 1956) and the Abalone Cove Landslide (active since ~1976). The primary tool was a network of dewatering wells that significantly reduced landslide movement.

Key Legal Instruments Referenced

Horan Agreement (October 13, 1987)

A global settlement between the City of RPV, the RPV Redevelopment Agency, and the County of Los Angeles resolving damage claims from the Abalone Cove Landslide. Section 7 set forth the purchase of Abalone Cove Beach (4 parcels, $1,060,000 — City paid $545,000, remaining $515,000 waived dollar-for-dollar against RDA assessment bond payments).

1975 LA County Condemnation

Los Angeles County filed a condemnation action to acquire two parcels for public purposes (Abalone Cove Beach): - APN 7572-019-901 (Sacred Cove Slope) - APN 7572-019-900 (Sacred Cove)

Any change in public use requires a new resolution of necessity (CCP § 1245.245).

Land and Water Conservation Fund Agreement (1975)

LA County acquired the coastal parcels using a federal Land and Water Conservation Fund grant. The agreement requires the properties be used for public outdoor recreation and prohibits conversion to other use without prior approval of both: 1. Director of CA Department of Parks and Recreation 2. Director of US Bureau of Outdoor Recreation (Dept of Interior)

The federal government may seek specific performance (not just monetary damages). Affects APNs: 7572-018-900 (Abalone Beach), 7572-019-901 (Sacred Cove Slope), 7572-019-900 (Sacred Cove).

Abalone Cove Ecological Reserve Agreement (1977)

LA County + CA Department of Fish and Game designated a portion of the coastal area as an ecological reserve for protecting rare/endangered wildlife and aquatic organisms. Affects: 7572-018-900 and 7572-019-900. Transferred with the land when County conveyed to RDA in 1987.

State Park Bond Act Grants (1986, 1988)

Over $200,000 (BB-19-349) in grants for parking lot and restrooms at Abalone Cove Shoreline Park. Restrictions: maintain in perpetuity per grant terms; some uses can only be changed with approval of the State Legislature.

Park Maintenance Easement Agreement (May 3, 2011)

RDA granted City a perpetual easement on all Shoreline Park parcels for park operations, construction, maintenance, roads, sewers, drainage, water, grading, and beachfront erosion prevention.

Archery Range Maintenance Easement (April 19, 2011)

RDA granted City a perpetual easement on the Archery Range parcel for grading to arrest land movement, repair public facilities, roads, sewers, drainage, water, and beachfront erosion.

NCCP (Natural Communities Conservation Plan)

City-prepared plan (approved in concept 2004, not yet finalized by state/federal agencies as of 2013). Proposes ~1,400-acre preserve for 10 covered species (6 plants, 4 animals) including California Gnatcatcher habitat (Coastal Sage Scrub). The Shoreline Park parcels are within the proposed NCCP Reserve.

LRPMP Amendment No. 1 (Resolution OB 2015-04)

Adopted September 9, 2015 (signed Ken Dyda, Chair; Kathryn Downs, Secretary). Adds an Exhibit A to the LRPMP covering RDA easement interests — agreements, covenants, and easements for drainage and sewer improvements. The Assignment and Assumption Agreement (Exhibit B) transfers all rights/obligations in these easements from the Successor Agency to the City.

RDA Easement Inventory

The amendment includes two massive indexes: 1. Drainage Improvements / Sanitary Sewers (Sept 1985 - Jan 1996): ~20 agreements/covenants with individual homeowners on Figtree Drive, Cinnamon Lane, Thyme Place, Narcissa Drive, Sweetbay Road, PV Drive South 2. Drainage, Monitoring Well, Sewer & Storm Drain (July 1984 - May 1998): ~25 easements for monitoring wells, drainage pipes, storm drains, sewer/storm drain lines 3. Abalone Cove Sewer Easements (1999-2002): ~200+ individual sewer and storm drain easements covering nearly every parcel in the Abalone Cove residential area (7572-001 through 7572-017 books), plus condemnation/lis pendens actions

Key entities: Palos Verdes Portuguese Bend LLC, Vanderlip family (multiple parcels), Portuguese Bend Riding Club, General Convention of the New Jerusalem (Wayfarer's Chapel), York Long Point.

DOF Approval Letter (April 25, 2014)

The CA Department of Finance actively reviewed and approved the LRPMP (correcting earlier assumption of deemed-approved-by-inaction). Key points from the letter signed by Justyn Howard, Assistant Program Budget Manager: - LRPMP submitted September 19, 2013 - Agency received Finding of Completion on April 26, 2013 - DOF approved use/disposition of all properties listed in the LRPMP - Per HSC § 34191.4, upon Finding of Completion + LRPMP approval, all property transfers to Community Redevelopment Property Trust Fund - Per HSC § 34191.3, the approved LRPMP governs and supersedes all other Dissolution Act provisions re: property - New agreements under the LRPMP still require OB approval per HSC § 34181(f)

Relevance to 0 Clipper / Cove

  1. APN 7573-007-900 is the key parcel — it sits in Parcel 106, the same remainder strip as 0 Clipper (7573-006-024). The city's CC&R acknowledgment in the LRPMP applies to land carved from the same parent parcel, in the same assessor book, with the same title history back to the 1920s. The LRPMP describes drainage via a 24-inch pipe under PVDS, but the parcel actually has an open creek — the same drainage feature that flows through or adjacent to 0 Clipper and makes that lot unbuildable. The city piped part of the watercourse and left the rest open, but it is the same creek.
  2. APN 7573-007-900 is zoned RS-2 — residential single-family despite being part of Shoreline Park. This is directly adjacent to 0 Clipper (7573-006-024). The LRPMP notes its residential zoning as a fact.
  3. CC&Rs are acknowledged — the LRPMP repeatedly states that CC&Rs recorded against the coastal parcels could limit development or use. The Successor Agency's own staff recognized CC&R restrictions.
  4. Federal and state encumbrances lock the Shoreline Park parcels into public recreation use in perpetuity — the federal LWCF agreement, the Ecological Reserve, the State Park Bond grants, and the NCCP Reserve.
  5. The RDA never owned 0 Clipper (7573-006-024) — that parcel is not in the LRPMP inventory. The RDA's 11 parcels are all adjacent or nearby, but 0 Clipper was always privately held.
  6. Landslide Moratorium Ordinance applies to all Cherryhill parcels — no new residences allowed. The LRPMP documents the City's own recognition that the entire area is geologically constrained.
  7. The drainage/sewer easement inventory (OB 2015-04 amendment) documents the RDA's massive infrastructure investment in the Abalone Cove area — hundreds of individual easements for sewer and storm drain improvements across the residential neighborhood.
