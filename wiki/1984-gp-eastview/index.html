COAC: 1984 GP Amendment 14 — Eastview Annexation (Jurisdictional-Continuity Precedent)

COAC: 1984 GP Amendment 14 — Eastview Annexation (Jurisdictional-Continuity Precedent)

Why this is in the COAC wiki even though Eastview is geographically opposite to Abalone Cove: the Eastview annexation is the cleanest local proof that pre-incorporation jurisdictional arrangements survive city annexation. That principle is the legal taproot of the coac reactivation framework — the WPBCA 1949 Declarations recorded under LA County jurisdiction survived RPV's 1973 incorporation by the same mechanism.

What the Document Is

RPV General Plan Amendment No. 14, adopted by City Council on August 7, 1984 (Resolution 84-48). Cover memorandum from Alice Angus (Environmental Services Dept.) to City Council, Planning Commission, Department Heads, and Environmental Services Staff, dated October 22, 1984.

"The General Plan Amendment deals exclusively with the Eastview Area which was annexed [in] 1983. The Amendment provides description of the new area, and changes [that] establish goals and policies to consider the unique characteristics of the area." — Alice Angus, Oct 22, 1984

CEQA: Environmental Assessment No. 450 (Initial Study and Negative Declaration).

Geography (Verified with Local Knowledge)

Eastview is the canyon east of Crenshaw Boulevard on the north side of the Palos Verdes Peninsula. The "Eastview Gate" is one of the access points into Rolling Hills. Western Avenue forms one of its eastern boundaries; bordered by San Pedro, Harbor City, Wilmington, and Lomita on the LA City / unincorporated side.

This is opposite to Abalone Cove (which sits on the south/west coastal edge of RPV). The Vanderlip-era Palos Verdes Project (1913) and its Declaration 100 / 101 (1929) do not reach Eastview — Eastview was never part of Vanderlip's syndicate lands.

Annexation Timeline

  • 1962–1969: Earliest Peninsula incorporation attempts
  • Sept 19, 1972: Curtis v. Board of Supervisors (Cal. Sup. Ct., 7-0) — landowners cannot prevent voters from determining municipal government
  • Aug 28, 1973: RPV incorporation election (5:1 in favor)
  • Sept 7, 1973: First RPV City Council meets
  • 1978: Eastview-area residents petition for RPV annexation
  • Nov 6, 1979: Eastview annexation election ("residents voted overwhelmingly in favor")
  • 1983: Legal annexation completes
  • Aug 7, 1984: GP-A14 adopted to incorporate Eastview into RPV's General Plan

The Jurisdictional-Continuity Anomalies (the Cove-Relevant Findings)

Even after annexation in 1983, the Eastview area retains at least these anomalies documented in the 1984 GP-A14 itself:

1. School District (LAUSD vs PVPUSD)

"Crestwood Elementary and Dodson Jr. High. Both schools are part of the Los Angeles Unified School District. Dodson, which has an enrollment of 1617 students, accepts students from portions of Lomita (south of Pacific Coast Highway), Harbor City (south of Anaheim Street), Wilmington (west of Fries Avenue between Anaheim and Street), San Pedro (west of the Harbor Freeway and north of 1st Street), and the entire Eastview area."

"The Palos Verdes Peninsula Unified School District does not include any of this area. It is not solely within [PVPUSD]."

"For [a school-district] transfer to take place, the area residents would need to petition the Los Angeles Unified Schools District. [The] process [is] difficult, although not necessarily impossible…"

RPV-incorporated, LAUSD-served. The annexation didn't change the school district.

2. Census tract pre-1983

"In 1970, the census tract for the unincorporated area included Eastview and portions of Lomita and Los Angeles City."

The unincorporated-area census tract crossed jurisdictional lines, mixing future-Eastview with future-Lomita-incorporated and LA-City lots. Some of those tract-level groupings persist in subsequent census geographies.

3. Public services

  • LA County Fire Department (Miraleste + Lomita stations) serves Eastview.
  • US Postal Service "recently opened an Annex" in PV.
  • Bus-stop locations remain "outside the City limits" including PVPUSD and Peninsula Center Library.

The Methodology Note (User Insight, 2026-04-19)

"The school district has to do with tax records and it's left over that is probably still covered by some declarations if you dig deep enough." — Project lead, 2026-04-19

This is a forensic methodology, not just an Eastview observation:

Use jurisdictional anomalies as a search heuristic for surviving pre-incorporation declarations and covenants. Where school district / tax / postal / utility / fire-district boundaries diverge from current city limits, look for buried county-era instruments. The deeper you dig the title chain, the more likely you find restrictions still in force.

Why This Document Strengthens the COAC Reactivation Argument

The COAC reactivation framework relies on the proposition that the 1929 Declaration 100 / 1949 WPBCA Declaration / 1950 Lot H Declaration are still in force despite RPV's 1973 incorporation. The Eastview precedent is direct evidence that:

  1. RPV annexation does not automatically extinguish pre-existing jurisdictional arrangements.
  2. School district + fire + postal + utility boundaries can persist across decades of subsequent city-level reorganization.
  3. The default rule is continuity, not extinguishment — the burden is on whoever wants to extinguish a pre-existing arrangement to show explicit statutory or judicial extinction.

By the same logic: WPBCA Tract 14649 was subdivided in 1949 under LA County jurisdiction; the recorded Declaration No. One (1949), Lot H Declaration (1950), Declaration of Easements (1949), and Declaration No. One-A (1950) were all recorded under county jurisdiction; none of these were extinguished by RPV's 1973 incorporation or any subsequent annexation, rezoning, or General Plan amendment.

Open Research Threads

  • Other Peninsula county pockets? LA County has ~140 county pockets countywide. Verify against current RPV city-limits GIS whether any unincorporated Peninsula parcels remain. Not derivable from this 1984 document.
  • Individual-lot anomalies? Worth checking parcel-by-parcel for lots in RPV / Rolling Hills / PVE that retained county-jurisdiction tax addressing or LAUSD assignment outside Eastview.
  • Inverse: Are there lots within nominally-LAUSD areas that retained PVPUSD assignment? Each anomaly is a candidate for surviving covenants.
  • Tax-roll analysis: Pull the LA County Assessor tax-roll for Tract 14649 and adjacent tracts; compare assessment-collection jurisdiction history pre- and post-1973.
