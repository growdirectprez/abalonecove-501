Coastal Specific Plan — Subregion 4

The City of Rancho Palos Verdes Coastal Specific Plan, adopted in 1978, is the city's certified Local Coastal Program under the California Coastal Act. Subregion 4 covers the Community of Abalone Cove — approximately 40 acres, 81 lots, five streets. This is the regulatory framework that governs 0 Clipper Road.

What It Says

Policies (S4-14, verbatim)

  1. Require all new housing within the confines of Subregion 4 to be of a single-family nature.
  2. Require development abutting the natural drainage course to maintain the natural character of the course.
  3. Require that the subdivision of large parcels within the confines of Subregion 4 be designed in a manner which will blend with the existing community pattern.
  4. Encourage lots developed in the confines of Subregion 4, yet outside the jurisdiction of West Portuguese Bend Community Association, to develop attached CC&Rs which parallel those presently enforced by the Association. Also, consideration should be given to annexation of these lots by the West Portuguese Bend Community Association.
  5. Study the existing drainage interface and its associated adverse impacts, and if found necessary, implement corrective measures.
  6. Continue to work with the residents and the County to solve the parking problems caused by Abalone Cove Beach Park.

Density

  • General Plan designated 2-4 D.U./AC. across all buildable area
  • Coastal Specific Plan lowered density to 2 D.U./AC. seaward of Sea Cove Drive because lots are .7-.8 acres with Open Space Hazard on rear, leaving buildable area less than actual lot size (S4-9)
  • Coastal Plan reduces total units by ~20 below General Plan — "primarily a reflection of lowering the density along the bluff" (S4-11)

Fourplexes at Clipper Road

Four fourplexes (16 multi-family units) at the western corner of PV Drive South and Clipper Road. Built 1962-1963. The Plan designates them as nonconforming and states: "It is, therefore, the intention of this Coastal Specific Plan to encourage the reversion of these apartments into a single-family area. Both lot size and orientation of the four fourplex parcels lend themselves to this conversion." (S4-9)

Natural Environment / Hazard Areas

  • 15 acres designated Open Space Hazard (Table S4-D)
  • Natural Environment/Hazard areas: "no development shall be allowed. Density credit will be given only for areas proven to the City's satisfaction to be stable." (U-2)
  • Boundaries are "uniquely dynamic (changing) character" — must be assessed by field inspection and historical aerial photography, not fixed lines (C-12)

Drainage

  • "A major natural drainage course exists in the east and serves as a natural division between Subregions 4 and 5." (S4-2)
  • The drainage course and eastern bluff identified as "habitat area in excellent condition and worthy of preserving" (S4-2)
  • "If upslope development occurs, drainage flow may increase creating a greater erosion factor" (S4-2)
  • "Two major canyons, each with flash flood potential, drain into Subregion 4. Future development adjacent to these canyons will no doubt create increased runoff." (S4-11)

Avoidance Corridors (Corridor Element)

  • Natural drainage courses are mapped as natural corridors (Figure 29)
  • Protection/Preservation corridors are defined as "avoidance" corridors — human activities must be excluded or stringently controlled (C-15)
  • "No planning or design criteria apply to these areas except those which would impact due to actions in adjacent areas" (C-15)
  • Category 1A geologic areas = potentially hazardous for human passage (C-15)
  • Natural corridors provide "important utilitarian functions such as drainage, etc., within an existing environment which does not have to be altered" (C-12)

Fire Station 53

  • Located on PV Drive South near Clipper Road, within Subregion 4
  • Response time ~30 seconds to center of subregion (S4-14)
  • Fire Department noted location and size will become inadequate; proposed relocation to PV Drive South and Forrestal Drive area (S4-14)
  • Schematic Plan #2: site should become recreational or civic use; if infeasible, revert to single-family residential (S4-10)
  • Station sits on Parcel 106 land — same remainder strip as 0 Clipper Road

Archaeological

  • Entire subregion designated with overlay control district for "archaeologically sensitive" sites (S4-5)
  • Significance level: HIGH (Table S4-B)

CC&Rs and Community

  • WPBCA CC&Rs cover 71% of the subregion (S4-5)
  • Plan encourages annexation of new lots into WPBCA (Policy 4)
  • "All new development will be residential single-family dwellings" (S4-10)

What the City Did in 2024

The city rezoned 0 Clipper Road from RS-4 to RM-22 — single-family to multi-family, twenty-two units per acre. Three ordinances in sixty-three days (April-June 2024). Sixteen units proposed on 1.56 acres.

Every element of this action conflicts with the certified Coastal Specific Plan:

Coastal Specific Plan Says Rezoning Does
All new housing single-family (Policy 1) Multi-family, 16 units
2 D.U./AC. seaward of Sea Cove (S4-9) 22 D.U./AC. (RM-22)
Drainage must maintain natural character (Policy 2) 16 units on a creek
Blend with existing community (Policy 3) Townhomes in SFR neighborhood
Fourplexes should revert to SFR (S4-9) Adds more multi-family
No density credit without proven stability (U-2) On an active landslide
Avoidance corridors — no development (C-15) Development on drainage course
Boundaries are dynamic, use historical data (C-12) Density based on snapshot parcel size

The Flag Pole Amendment

The Coastal Specific Plan was formally amended (LCP amendment, Coastal Commission certified) to add a flag pole height variance provision for the Trump National Golf Course. The amendment text is laser-printed — visibly different typeface from the original 1978 typewriter text. Detailed conditions: 120-acre minimum parcel, publicly dedicated land, American flag only, 20 free parking spaces, restrooms, fountain, bench seating. (C-12)

A flag pole required an LCP amendment. Sixteen condos on a landslide did not.

The Proposal

A Coastal Specific Plan-compliant solution for 0 Clipper:

  • Creek/flood hazard (0.57 ac) → permanent open space, habitat preservation, avoidance corridor
  • Buildable remainder (~1 ac) → four SFR lots per 1980 Wong subdivision (Tract 32977), RS-4, WPBCA annexation
  • Existing fourplexes at Clipper/PV Drive South → reclassify from nonconforming to conforming, designate as affordable/low-income housing (satisfies RHNA without new construction on unstable land)
  • Fire station site → civic/recreational per Schematic Plan #2

Same number of housing units. No construction on active landslide. Every element from the city's own certified plan.

Implementation Partners

The Sub Region 4 proposal requires three organizations working together:

Partner Role What They Bring
WPBCA (HOA) Governance CC&R enforcement standing, annexation mechanism (Policy 4), 81-lot membership, beach path easements
Abalone Cove Foundation (501(c)(3)) Advocacy Public record, research, legal opinion funding, tax-deductible donations, the article
PVPLC (PV Peninsula Land Conservancy) Preservation Conservation easement expertise, adjacent Portuguese Bend Reserve management, FEMA/state agency relationships, land trust capacity

The creek preservation, fire station parcel, and bluff habitat protection all require a land trust partner. PVPLC already manages thousands of acres on the peninsula. Adding the 0.57-acre creek/flood hazard area is a natural extension of their existing conservation boundary adjacent to Abalone Cove Park.
