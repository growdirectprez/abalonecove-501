CSUDH Palos Verdes Field Guide (McNulty, 2012)

Educational field guide by Dr. Brendan McNulty (Dept. of Earth Science, CSUDH), funded by the CSUDH Presidential Creative Initiative Fund. Written for non-geologists, it covers a self-guided 4-hour driving/walking field trip from CSUDH through five coastal stops on the Palos Verdes Peninsula.

What It Covers

The guide takes students from LA Harbor south along the PV coast through Point Fermin (Sunken City), Royal Palms/White Point, Terranea, the Portuguese Bend landslide, Bluff Cove, and Malaga Cove. At each stop, McNulty explains visible geological features with photos and accessible explanations.

Key Geological Content

Monterey Formation

The dominant bedrock of the PV coast. Miocene age (~8-15 Ma), deposited in deep marine environment (mile+ water depth). Subdivided into three members: Altamira Shale, Valmonte Diatomite, and Malaga Mudstone. Oil-bearing. The rocks are inherently weak -- mudstone and shale that can be broken by hand.

Bentonite as Slide Surface

McNulty explicitly identifies bentonite clay layers within the formation as planes of weakness that act as slide surfaces. Combined with ocean-ward bedding dip and rain infiltration, bentonite is a primary mechanism for PV landslides.

Seven Causes of PV Coastal Landslides

McNulty lists these as a group (p. 8): 1. Wave erosion over-steepens cliffs 2. Ocean creates a "free face" 3. Gravity 4. Inherently weak rocks (mudstone/shale) 5. Bedding dips toward ocean (~10 degrees at Pt. Fermin) 6. Bentonite clay layers act as slide surfaces 7. Rain infiltration lubricates slide surfaces

Portuguese Bend Landslide

  • Dimensions: ~1/2 mile wide, 3/4 mile long
  • Active for ~250,000 years
  • Modern reactivation: 1950s post-WWII building boom
  • Cause of modern acceleration: groundwater rise from irrigation, pools, septic tanks
  • Movement rate (2012): ~14 feet/year
  • Dewatering pipes parallel the road to remove water from slopes
  • Water/sewage lines placed above ground so sliding can continue beneath
  • Road requires annual re-patching

Marine Terraces

13 marine terraces identified on PV Peninsula, created by sequential tectonic uplift along the Palos Verdes fault. McNulty notes that most PV residential development sits on uplifted marine terraces because they provide flat building surfaces. Three tiers of terraces are visible from Malaga Cove beach.

Palos Verdes Fault

Oblique-slip fault trending NW along the NE side of PV, from LA Harbor to south Torrance. Strike-slip to dip-slip ratio of 10:1. Responsible for creating the PV hills, uplifting the Monterey Formation from marine to terrestrial setting, and generating all 13 marine terraces. Root cause: transpression from the left bend in the San Andreas fault.

Development vs. Geological Hazards

McNulty repeatedly raises the tension between residential development and geological reality: - Rhetorical caption "Wise place to build a house?" under photos of homes on eroding cliffs - Post-WWII development directly triggered modern Portuguese Bend sliding through water infiltration - Trump National Golf Course hole collapse cited as ongoing instability example - Ancient landslide scarps visible beneath existing Malaga Cove buildings - 2011 Paseo del Mar road collapse documented with AP aerial photos
