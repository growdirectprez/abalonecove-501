Case-Law Synthesis — Rules That Bind WPBCA

Cross-cutting synthesis of every court decision and active filing that currently shapes WPBCA's posture. This is the "rules ledger" — each entry is a rule, a holding, or a controlling authority, distilled from the cited sources, tagged by what it controls.

This article does not re-summarize each case (see the linked wiki articles). It answers: given this body of case law, what are we actually allowed or required to do?

Rules that bind us — private CC&R domain

R1 — CC&Rs are presumed valid; the burden falls on the challenger.

  • Authority: Nahrstedt v. Lakeside Village (1994) 8 Cal. 4th 361.
  • How it applies: When WPBCA enforces a recorded restriction on Tract 14649, the challenger must prove unreasonableness. WPBCA does not need to justify the restriction's rationality ab initio.
  • Source: cove legal framework.

R2 — A recorded declaration binds all subsequent purchasers on actual or constructive notice.

  • Authority: Citizens for Covenant Compliance v. Anderson (1995) 12 Cal. 4th 345.
  • How it applies: The 1949 Declaration No. One + 1949 Declaration of Easements + 1950 Declaration One-A + 1952 grant deed chain (see cove legal framework) are binding on current Tract 14649 owners because they appear in the chain of title.
  • Corollary (from Colyear): If the restriction is not in the chain of title, Citizens doesn't save it — extrinsic evidence of original intent is insufficient. See R5.

R3 — CC&Rs run with the land and bind successor owners.

  • Authority: Villa De Las Palmas v. Terifaj (2004) 33 Cal. 4th 73.
  • How it applies: Tract 14649 restrictions are enforceable against any current owner regardless of knowledge, as long as they are in the chain of title per R2.

R4 — PV Corp deed restrictions are enforceable as equitable servitudes.

  • Authority: Russell v. Palos Verdes Properties (1963) 218 Cal. App. 2d 754.
  • Status after Colyear (2024): Russell's core holding (equitable enforcement against transferees with notice) is still good law. What Colyear narrowed is Russell's dicta about the general-plan theory — PV Corp's intent does not override defective recording. See cove colyear 2024.
  • How it applies: Tract 14649 restrictions (properly recorded in the chain of title) are enforceable. Declaration 100 / 101 enforcement against parcels outside the chain of title is now doubtful — see R5 + R6.

R5 — A master declaration does not bind a property unless the chain of title clearly incorporates it.

  • Authority: Colyear v. Rolling Hills Community Assn (2024) Case B308382 (2nd App. Dist., Div. 4, March 1, 2024, certified for partial publication).
  • Holding: Declaration 150 (1936) did not apply to Colyear's property because Declaration 150-M (1944) did not clearly and unequivocally incorporate Dec. 150. Kelvin Vanderlip's testimony + Russell general-plan dicta were insufficient; constructive notice requires proper recording.
  • How it applies to Declaration 100: Declaration 100 probably does not apply to 0 Clipper Road (APN 7573-006-024) because the CTC title report shows no Declaration 100 or 101 in the chain of title. Page 155 of Declaration 100 (property description) is smeared / illegible, so even if it covers the whole of Lot H, the court requires the restriction to be in the chain of title OR a clear, unequivocal incorporation by reference.
  • Source: cove colyear 2024; the 1947 Kelvin Vanderlip admission — "Practically all of the provisions which were contained in Declaration No. 150… were removed by our attorneys as far back as the Flying Triangle Declaration [(Declaration 160)] for the purpose of simplification" — is the single most devastating piece of testimony in the record and belongs in any PVC-scheme analysis.

R6 — Extrinsic evidence of original intent does not cure recording defects.

  • Authority: Colyear (2024) following Citizens (1995).
  • How it applies: WPBCA cannot rely on what PV Corp meant to cover with Declaration 100. What's on the paper, recorded properly, is what binds. This cuts both ways — it's why Tract 14649 is well-protected (clean chain) and why Lot H outside the Tract is not (cove lot h discovery, claim lot h scope correction).

R7 — Racial covenants are void; the remainder of a declaration may still stand.

  • Authority: Cal. Civ. Code § 12955 (supersedes Shelley v. Kraemer).
  • How it applies: The 1950 Lot H Declaration's only substantive restriction was a racial exclusionary covenant. It is void. Since there is no non-racial substantive content in that instrument, Lot H Declaration provides no enforceable building controls. This is a canonical correction captured in claim lot h scope correction. Architectural controls in the 2009 Restated Declaration trace back to Declaration No. One (1949), which applies only to Tract 14649.

R8 — No current California housing law preempts pre-existing CC&Rs for this project.

  • Authority: Statutory analysis in cove legal framework.
  • How it applies: Housing Element / RHNA, Builder's Remedy (§ 65589.5), SB 9, AB 721, Civil Code § 4751, AB 1050, and the Coastal Act all constrain government permits — they do not override private restrictive covenants.
  • Caveat — case of first impression: No California court has directly ruled on whether pre-existing CC&Rs can block Housing Element rezoning. WPBCA enforcement of Tract 14649 CC&Rs against a Builder's Remedy project would be novel. Plan accordingly.

R9 — Equitable servitudes persist unless clearly extinguished by an explicit recorded instrument.

  • Authority: Cal. Civ. Code § 5975(a); amendment/termination procedures in §§ 4260–4270; judicial-termination doctrine (abandonment, changed circumstances, Nahrstedt unreasonableness).
  • How it applies to WPBCA: Once a recorded equitable servitude runs with the land, only specific mechanisms extinguish it:
  • Member-approved amendment/restatement following the declaration's own procedures and Davis-Stirling rules
  • Expiration by the declaration's own terms (unless extended per Civ §4265)
  • Judicial termination on a clear evidentiary showing
  • Statutory override (narrow — e.g., discriminatory covenants voided by Civ §12955; solar/EV exceptions)
  • What does NOT extinguish a servitude: ambiguity; silence in a later deed; subdivision; reversion; state or city involvement in a title transfer that does not expressly terminate the servitude with proper authority and recording; inclusion/exclusion in a deed without a compliant amendment; informal waiver not rising to abandonment.
  • Course-of-conduct evidence (2026-04-21 finding): PV Corp and WPBCA demonstrably knew how to use the recording apparatus explicitly when a legal status change was intended. Specifically:
  • 1949-12-16 Doc 2519 — PV Corp recorded a Statement of Suspension of Authority explicitly invoking Dec. No. One Art. V §3(h), specifying a precise 2-year term ending Dec 10, 1951, and reserving the right to shorten the period by another recording. See founding/1949-12-16-pv-corp-suspension-of-wpbca-assessment-authority-doc-2519.pdf.
  • 1950-06-08 Utility Easement Inst 2370 — PV Corp → Southern California Edison + Associated Telephone; explicit recorded easement for electric/telephone utility lines across named strip footprints on 48+ lots of Tract 14649. Another example of explicit recorded property action.
  • 1950-12-26 Doc 1182 — ARC members appointment declaration, explicit exercise of the appointment power.
  • 1954-04-13 Doc 3529 — subsequent ARC members appointment, again explicit.
  • 1959 Doc 3150 / 1961 Doc 4334 — WPBCA/PV Corp easement-modification chain: every change recorded, metes-and-bounds stated, reciprocal instruments executed.

  • 2005 Wedemeyer/Provident Title research — documented exhaustive use of recording apparatus across decades.

  • Supporting contemporary evidence — the City's own administrative practice: In the 2012-05-16 Notice of Decision ZON2011-00266 (pool/grading permit for 12 Barkentine), RPV's Community Development Department explicitly conditioned the permit on non-encroachment into Instrument No. 2856, recorded May 10, 1949 — a 63-year-old easement. The decision's Condition 18 requires either (a) written authorization from the legal holder, or (b) a court judgment of extinguishment, before any construction inside the easement footprint. No "city-deemed-inoperative" third option. This is the city's own formal process recognizing that servitudes persist unless explicitly extinguished — and therefore internally inconsistent with later actions (e.g., 2024 rezoning of 0 Clipper to RM-22) that proceed as if prior recorded restrictions simply don't apply. See city-records/permit-records/2012-05-16-rpv-notice-of-decision-zon2011-00266-12-barkentine-griffis-pool-grading.pdf.

  • The affirmative inference: The absence of any similarly explicit recorded extinguishment for the Tract 14649 CC&R chain, the Ocean Access Easements, or Declaration 100/101 (where applicable) is affirmative evidence that those servitudes were not extinguished. Silence in an upstream deed, a state conveyance, or a subdivision map is legally insufficient to terminate a servitude — and course-of-conduct evidence shows the parties would have recorded an explicit extinguishment if one had been intended.
  • How to argue it: In any enforcement or defense context (including Case 24TRCP00352 and future CC&R enforcement against a Builder's Remedy project), pair Civ §5975(a) with the archival record of explicit recordings to establish the negative — that no extinguishment recording exists, and the parties would have made one if they had intended termination.
  • Source: cove legal framework; cove 0 clipper; coac reactivation framework.

Rules that bind us — public/regulatory domain

R9 — Inverse condemnation liability attaches to the public entity without proof of negligence, where a public improvement causes direct damage to private property.

  • Authority: Albers v. County of Los Angeles (1965) 62 Cal. 2d 250. Judgments aggregating $5.36 million affirmed against LA County for damages caused by the 1956 Portuguese Bend landslide, which was triggered by 175,000 cubic yards of fill placed in a road easement and adjacent lands during the Crenshaw Boulevard extension. Recovery under Art. I, §14 of the CA Constitution ("private property shall not be taken or damaged for public use without just compensation").
  • How it applies: The landslide is the county's inverse-condemnation predicate. This is bedrock precedent for the Portuguese Bend area. The CA Supreme Court rejected the county's argument that inverse-condemnation liability requires a parallel common-law tort — the constitutional "or damaged" provision is an independent cause of action.
  • Strategic implication for WPBCA: Any future public-improvement project in the moratorium area (road work, drainage, emergency access) that causes further land movement or property damage creates a fresh Albers claim. This is leverage — and a warning. A public entity cannot plead innocence where it knew the prehistoric slide plane existed (federal geological report was public by 1946 per the Albers opinion).
  • Gap: No dedicated wiki article exists yet — creating cove albers 1965 is a real coverage gap and should be prioritized.

R10 — Geological hazard disclosure is the baseline; ignorance is not available to the city.

  • Authority: Albers opinion (1965) — court noted that the 1946 federal geological report on the Portuguese Bend prehistoric slide plane "was known to both the county and the developers."
  • How it applies: RPV cannot claim "we didn't know" about landslide hazard. The baseline is cemented in primary literature (see coac 1967 portuguese bend clay mineralogy), the 1975 GP (coac 1975 general plan eir), the 1990/2001 HEs (coac rpv housing elements), and the 2018 Safety Element (cove rpv safety element 2018). Any city action inconsistent with this 80-year hazard record runs into R10.

R11 — Landslide moratorium legitimates a "not buildable" designation against RHNA pressure.

  • Authority: Monks v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes (2008) 167 Cal. App. 4th 263. Upheld RPV's landslide moratorium framework.
  • How it applies: The moratorium is a legally defensible limit on development — not a workaround. The 2001 Housing Element's "not buildable" inventory entries are consistent with Monks. State RHNA pressure in 2024 does not automatically erase Monks's authority.
  • See also: cove property geology, cove zone2 geotech 2011.

R12 — Geological problems can kill a land deal — Phillippe v. Shapell precedent.

  • Authority: Phillippe v. Shapell (1987) 43 Cal. 3d 1247. A 1973 attempt to purchase Filiorum Corporation land failed "due to geological problems."
  • How it applies: Historical precedent for walking away from Portuguese Bend / Filiorum land because of geology. Shapell walked in 1973; Clipper Development bought adjacent land 51 years later. Different answer, same geology. cove colyear 2024 highlights this contrast.

R13 — View ordinances can be a regulatory taking — at least the argument is preserved.

  • Authority: Echevarrieta v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes (2001) SCOTUS cert petition. The underlying CA Court of Appeal ruling (Division 4) held against the petitioner; cert presumably denied (per coac rpv scotus petitions).
  • Status: Takings argument against RPV view ordinance is not settled at the federal level, but has not succeeded either. The issue remains live — any new enforcement that requires destruction of mature trees to grant views to another property owner is a Fifth/Fourteenth Amendment takings vector.
  • Modern bookend: Colyear (2024) is the private-covenant analog. Together they delineate what's enforceable under each authority.

R14 — Builder's Remedy applies only to city zoning, not private covenants.

  • Authority: Gov. Code § 65589.5; statutory analysis in cove legal framework.
  • How it applies: Builder's Remedy lets a developer bypass non-compliant city zoning. It does not affect properly recorded CC&Rs (R2, R3, R4, R8). A BR-approved project on Lot H land can still be subject to WPBCA's Tract 14649 CC&Rs if the parcel is in the Tract.

Rules that bind us — secret-meeting / transparency domain

R15 — The "litigation-hold secret meetings between city and private parties" problem is a recognized constitutional vector.

  • Authority: Stuart v. RPV (1994) SCOTUS cert petition, Question 5: "Can local governments use the issue of litigation to hold secret meetings with private parties in order to devise [strategies] that could circumvent the law?"
  • Status: Cert presumably denied; no definitive SCOTUS ruling. But the issue was raised in 1994 by an RPV resident and is the same vector behind the 2024 Cruikshank FPPC complaint. Thirty-year resident frustration is now on the record.
  • How it applies to current posture: FPPC complaint vehicles, Brown Act challenges, and records-act requests all draw from this line. The historical continuity matters — this is not novel politics, it's a 30-year pattern.

R16 — Supremacy Clause is invoked as a check on city land-use authority.

  • Authority: Stuart v. RPV + Teng Lee v. RPV (SCOTUS cert petitions, 1990s).
  • How it applies: Two RPV residents independently argued federal-supremacy limits on city action. Now the state is using the same structural argument against the city (via HE / Builder's Remedy). The argument is the same; the party invoking it has changed. This is a narrative anchor, not an enforcement tool for WPBCA.

Rules about enforcement by WPBCA

R17 — WPBCA has enforcement standing for Tract 14649 restrictions.

  • Authority: 2009 Restated Declaration; 2012 Bylaws; Russell (R4); Nahrstedt (R1); Citizens (R2).
  • How it applies: Clean. Tract 14649 restrictions are enforceable; WPBCA has standing; burden is on the challenger.

R18 — WPBCA's standing for non-Tract-14649 parcels (Lot H, Lot H remainder, 0 Clipper) is doubtful.

  • Authority: Colyear (R5, R6); claim lot h scope correction; cove lot h discovery.
  • How it applies: Outside Tract 14649, WPBCA does not have a clean enforcement path unless the chain-of-title analysis identifies a different binding instrument.
  • Mitigation: Individual lot owner plaintiffs as backup per cove legal framework; public-regulatory framework (Coastal Specific Plan, Safety Element, ESA) as parallel tracks per cove rpv planning framework 2026 and cove endangered species constraints.

R19 — Ocean path easement is defensible but sensitive.

  • Authority: 1949 Declaration of Easements + 1952 grant deeds.
  • How it applies: The bridle trail / walkway from Lot 1 to the ocean is a private recorded easement. Coastal Commission public-access policies run parallel but do not replace the private instrument. This is a live governance matter — see wpbca ocean path easement (visibility: private governance matter) for the detailed posture.
