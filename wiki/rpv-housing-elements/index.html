COAC: RPV Housing Elements (1990, 2001) — Annotated for Cove

The 1990 and 2001 RPV Housing Elements are the city's formal state-mandated housing planning documents. Together they establish the city's pre-modern position on Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA), the use of the landslide moratorium as the binding constraint on housing capacity, and the inventory framework that the 2018 GP and 2024 Builder's Remedy fight inherited.

For Cove 501c, these are evidence that the city has consistently treated the Portuguese Bend / Abalone Cove area as land-banked through the moratorium — a position the city itself relied on for decades, now being walked back under state pressure.

1990 Housing Element — Highlights

  • Predecessor: GP Amendment 9 (Resolution 81-71, Sept 23, 1981) updated the housing-element policies in the original 1975 GP (changes to pp. 44, 56, 78). The 1990 doc is the first standalone Housing Element thereafter.
  • Cove-relevant counts: Abalone Cove (3), Portuguese Bend (7), landslide (39), landslide moratorium (15), coastal zone (13), RHNA (12), regional housing needs (10).
  • Coastal Zone Requirements has its own Progress Report sub-section (Element II.E in the TOC) — explicit Coastal Act compliance reporting.
  • Establishes the long-running RPV pattern: landslide moratorium + coastal zone = the binding constraints, private-association covenants invisible.

Landslide Moratorium = "Not Buildable" — On the City's Own Record

The 2001 Housing Element's sites inventory explicitly lists the moratorium area as "Not buildable". Specific Cove-relevant inventory entries:

  • "Portugese Bend [sic] 230 OH, In landslide moratorium / Steep slopes"
  • "RDA Property in Portugese [Bend] 10 acres, vacant. Redevelopment Agency owns in landslide moratorium area."
  • "Limited access, steep slopes, geologic concerns, uncertain development potential / Mostly in Landslide Moratorium / City owned / Open space preserve / Not buildable"

The city formally ranked moratorium parcels as zero RHNA capacity in 2001. That position is at the heart of the current 0 Clipper Road / Builder's Remedy tension — the state is now forcing rezoning of parcels the city itself has long classified as unbuildable.

Named Planning Instruments

The 2001 HE references two formal instruments that remain in city code:

  • Landslide Moratorium Exclusion Permit — Staff Review level
  • Landslide Moratorium Exception Permit — City Council level

Both are quotable in any Restated Declaration recital framing the moratorium as a substantive entitlement constraint, not a mere technical filing.

Cove 501c Strategic Implications

  1. Estoppel-style argument: The city relied on the moratorium-as-not-buildable framing for ≥30 years (1990, 2001 HEs explicit). Reversal under state pressure invites legal challenge — Cove can cite the city's own HEs against any 0 Clipper-style upzoning.
  2. Private-covenant gap: Neither HE acknowledges the WPBCA Tract 14649 covenants, the Lot H Declaration, or the Declaration of Easements. The city's housing-capacity calculations have always silently assumed the private restrictive framework would not be enforced. Reactivating Declaration 100/101 reverses that assumption.
  3. RDA legacy: The 10-acre RDA-owned Portuguese Bend parcel + LRPMP (2013) + RDA dissolution (2012) chain is documented here. See cove rpv redevelopment conveyance.
